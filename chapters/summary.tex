\chapter{Summary}

Peat is widely used in growing media, but its extraction damages peatlands, and biochar is one of the materials proposed to replace it. In this study, the effect of biochar as a peat alternative on the salt stress response of three mint taxa, \textit{Mentha piperita}, \textit{Mentha crispata} and \textit{Mentha spicata} `Moroccan', was examined.

The experiment was carried out in a growth chamber with 27 pots (one pot per combination), each containing 400~g of substrate with three to four plants. Each taxon was grown in unamended peat and in peat in which 15 or 30~g of the peat was replaced by biochar, and received 0, 25 or 50~mM NaCl once a week for three weeks. Four weeks after the first application, plant height, leaf size, Fv/Fm ratio, chlorophyll content and relative water content were measured on leaves from the upper third of the plants. The effects were described and tested within each taxon by two-way analysis of variance on the pot means.

Salinity reduced plant height, leaf size and chlorophyll content in all taxa. In unamended peat, plant height at 50~mM was 31\% lower than at 0~mM in \textit{M. piperita}, 44\% lower in \textit{M. crispata} and 51\% lower in \textit{M. spicata} `Moroccan', which was the most sensitive taxon. The Fv/Fm ratio decreased only slightly. Biochar increased plant height and leaf size at every salinity level and reduced the loss of growth and chlorophyll caused by salt, the effect being greater with 30~g than with 15~g and in the more sensitive taxa. The relative water content gave results that could not be explained and need to be checked.

It is concluded that biochar can partly replace peat in the growing media of mint and can reduce the effect of salt stress. Because only one pot was available for each combination, the results are preliminary and should be confirmed in replicated experiments.
