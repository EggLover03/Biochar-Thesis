\chapter{Literature Review}

\section{Importance of mint as a medicinal and spice plant}

\subsection{Mint (\textit{Mentha spp.})}
Mint species have economic importance and are widely used in various industrial sectors (food, pharmaceutical and cosmetic) for their medicinal and aromatic properties \textcite{Zheljazkov2020}. Of these, \textit{Mentha piperita}, \textit{M. spicata} and \textit{M. crispata} are of particular interest because of their capacity of essential oil production and biomass production.

Mint is a herb of great medicinal value since ancient times. In the historical documents of Nicander of Colophon \textcite{Silva2020}, mint is used for making a repellant stench to repel snakes. It suggests that mint is a part of traditional medicine and has a significant place in therapeutic uses.

In addition, mint is also valued for its essential oil composition. The essential oils in peppermint leave consist of 1.2–3.9\% of total peppermint leaves and more than 300 compounds have been identified. Among these, the terpenic class is the most abundant, accounting for about 52\% monoterpenes and 9\% sesquiterpenes, while other classes like aldehyde (9\%), alcohol (6\%), miscellaneous compounds (8\%), lactone (7\%) and aromatic hydrocarbons (9\%) are present in lower proportions \textcite{Silva2020}. Mint (\textit{Mentha spp.}) is mostly perennial, an aromatic herb, botanically. It is well known as a medicinal and edible herb. \textit{Mentha spp.} have been applied since ancient civilizations and continue to be relevant in the field of medicine and traditional health systems as well as in the food industry \textcite{Silva2020}.

\subsection{Mint as a medicine plant}
Mint is a medicinal herb that has been utilized for a long time, especially as a digestive tonic. \textit{Mentha} species have been reported to have carminative, antispasmodic, anti-emetic, antimicrobial, antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and insecticidal properties in previous studies. Its medicinal properties are primarily attributed to the essential oils and phenolic compounds including menthol, menthone, flavonoids and phenolic acids.

One of the most studied plants is peppermint (\textit{Mentha piperita}) which is used for gastrointestinal disorders including indigestion, flatulence, colic, nausea and irritable bowel syndrome. The antimicrobial action of the mint essential oils on various bacteria and fungi is a good justification for their traditional use in the treatment of infectious diseases and in food preservation. In addition, the soothing, cooling and antiseptic properties of mint oils and menthol make them very useful for pharmaceutical formulations, oral hygiene products, cosmetics and aromatherapy. Its medicinal uses are also highlighted, and mint has been used for medicinal purposes since ancient Babylonian, Greek, Roman, Islamic and medieval European times. Since \textcite{Silva2020} writes “Mints have been used since ancient Babylon, but it was in Classical Antiquity that their medical uses blossomed.” and given that the 500 BCE Greek physician Hippocrates used them, it is logical to assume that they’ve been utilized in medicine for quite some time.

\subsection{The importance of mint as a spice plant has increased.}
In addition to its medicinal properties, mint is also a significant spice crop for numerous cultures. The edible portions of the plant are the leaves which may be eaten fresh or dried; fresh mint is preferred over dried in most cases.

Fresh leaves are preferred because they have a stronger aroma and taste. Mint leaves taste warm, sweet and refreshing with a distinctive cooling sensation. They are therefore used extensively as a flavor in a variety of foods and drinks, including sauces, jellos, confectioneries, syrups, ice creams and traditional dishes. Mint is a key ingredient in Middle Eastern cooking, Indian, European and African cuisines, where it is used to enhance taste, and as a digestive aid. Mint EO's are also widely used in food industry due to their antimicrobial properties as natural flavoring agents and preservatives.

\subsection{Economic and cultural significance}
Mint is important for its medicinal and spice value, and is an economically important crop in many countries around the world. It is crucial for agriculture and industry to produce and process fresh and dried mint leaves. Today, mint is still used in medicine and food, cosmetics, and perfumery, highlighting the ongoing importance of mint as a versatile plant with global significance. According to \textcite{Silva2020}, “They are still being used for many purposes, including non-medicinal, therefore are of economic importance herbs.”

\section{Botanical and economic importance of \textit{Mentha piperita}, 
\textit{Mentha spicata}, and \textit{Mentha crispata}}

\textit{Mentha piperita} (peppermint) is one of the most important mints economically because of its content of menthol and menthone. Menthol gives a ``cooling effect'' and is a common ingredient of drugs, mouth fresheners, cosmetics, and food flavor.

\textit{Mentha spicata} (spearmint) has a large concentration of carvone in its essential oil. The mild and sweet taste of spearmint makes it a popular addition to the perfumery industry, flavoring industry, especially for chewing gum and toothpaste, beverages, and confectionery. \textit{Mentha crispata} (curly mint) is regarded as a variety of spearmint and is identified by its curled or wrinkled foliage.

It is a common ingredient in cooking, for salads, herbal teas and garnishes, and is also used in the production of essential oils.

\section{Peat moss for indoor and greenhouse mint culture.}

In greenhouse growing, peat moss is commonly used as a growing medium because of its favourable physical properties, but significant environmental impacts are related to peat moss extraction, such as carbon emissions and peatland degradation \parencite{Cleary2021}. Drained peatlands, used for horticulture, account for around a third of the world's soil carbon storage, but once drained become large emitters of CO\textsubscript{2}. Peatlands account for almost 5\% of the global CO\textsubscript{2} emissions, which is more than the emissions from international aviation and shipping.

Peat harvesting also results in the destruction of valuable habitats and biodiversity in the sensitive ecosystems of Canada's Hudson Bay Lowlands. Another issue is sustainability, as peat only grows very slowly (around 1 mm per year), but the soil is removed much more quickly, essentially a non-renewable resource. These environmental issues have led to regulatory measures such as the UK's policy to end peat in horticulture by 2030 and EU efforts to decrease peat reliance.

Therefore, biochar has been suggested as a sustainable alternative growing media ingredient because of its porous structure, chemical stability and ability to enhance growing media properties \textcite{Dumroese2018}. Even with its horticultural benefits however, the environmental considerations of peat moss use reinforce the need for alternative growing media.

\subsection{Overview and Economic Importance}

Species of \textit{Mentha} genus are of high economic value as containing active and aromatic substances and are used as raw material in food, cosmetics, health, beverage and pharmaceutical industries \parencite{yaghobvand2022evaluation}. As a high quality essential oil becomes more popular there is a need to understand better the factors that affect plant growth and physiological characteristics.

\section{Growth Requirements and Physiology of Mint}

\subsection{\textit{Mentha piperita} (Peppermint)}

\subsection*{Botanical Characteristics}

\textit{Mentha} × \textit{piperita} is a rhizomatous, upright perennial herbaceous plant. The \textcite{rhs2026mentha} states that peppermint is a hybrid formed by a cross between \textit{Mentha aquatica} (watermint) and \textit{Mentha spicata} (spearmint) and is regarded as a species but is now known to be a hybrid. It grows tall stems with dark green leaves and reddish stem with warm, spicy odor.

\subsection*{Requirements for climate and environment.}

\textcite{virzo1980adaptability} showed that growth in height of \textit{Mentha piperita} is superior at 44\% daylight as compared with 100\% daylight. They also found that at 14\% daylight growth is restricted, presumably due to insufficient photosynthate, and there is no flowering. Their transfer experiments between light and shade showed that peppermint is very responsive to irradiance not only at seedling stage (via specific leaf area and chlorophyll content) but also at a late stage of the life cycle. As far as temperature is concerned, environmental factors such as extreme temperatures can reduce yield and make the crop more susceptible to diseases. Some shade may be necessary in summer heat in some climates, according to the \textcite{umd2026growing}; in subtropical regions, summer heat can stunt plant growth if some shade is not provided.

The plant is considered an adequate moisture plant and will tolerate medium to wet soil conditions, according to the \textcite{mobot2026piperita}.

\subsection*{Physiological responses to nutrient stress}

The interaction between photosynthetic carbon metabolism and essential oil biogenesis in peppermint under manganese stress has also been studied. Mn deficiency significantly decreased CO\textsubscript{2} exchange rate, total chlorophyll, total assimilatory area, plant dry weight and essential oil yield while it significantly increased the Chl a/b, leaf area ratio and leaf stem ratio. CO\textsubscript{2} incorporation into primary metabolic pool and essential oil were significantly reduced in leaves of manganese deficient plants \parencite{srivastava1994relationship}.

Peppermint has also been studied with regards to iron deficiency. CO\textsubscript{2} exchange rate, stomatal conductance, oil content, chlorophyll content and chlorophyll a/b ratio were significantly impacted. Iron stress also affected growth parameters like the leaf area ratio, specific leaf weight, and leaf stem ratio \parencite{srivastava1993relation}.

\subsection*{Soil and Nutrient Requirements}

Peppermint prefers rich, moist soils, but will tolerate most soil types with the exception of dry soil \parencite{umd2026growing}. Good soil quality, including organic matter and available nutrients, is important to good growth.

\subsection{\textit{Mentha spicata} (Spearmint)}

\subsection*{Botanical Characteristics}

According to the \textcite{rhs2026spicata}, spearmint (\textit{Mentha spicata}) produces pointed, slightly crinkled leaves that are lighter green in color than peppermint, with the whole plant having a characteristic sweet smell. Aromatic perennial that spreads by creeping rhizomes.

\subsection*{The Physiological responses to water stress.}

A field experiment was conducted at Southern Italy on spearmint under various irrigation regimes to determine the effects of the progressive increase in water stress on later developing leaves, plant water status, and net photosynthesis, starting from the first period of water stress. The harvest period was significantly affected by limitation of photosynthesis. During the severe water stress condition, the crop reduced water loss by regulating stomatal closure and had reduced mesophyll conductance. The content of organic compounds was not affected by irrigation treatments, but the yield of essential oils was affected negatively by water stress because the growth of the crop, both total and leaf biomass, leaf area index and plant height was reduced \parencite{marino2019evaluation}.

\subsection*{In Vitro Culture Responses}

The research of \textcite{tisserat2004influence} on the influence of physical parameters on spearmint cultures in vitro indicated that physical support greatly influenced culture growth and morphogenesis. They found that, mint shootlets can be cultivated on liquid medium to yield four times more fresh weight than on the agar medium. There were positive relationships between growth, morphogenesis and carvone content with culture vessel size. By using an automated plant culture system, the biomass produced was about 15 times more than that of culture tubes.

These results were corroborated by more recent studies by \textcite{tisserat2008growth} which showed that carvone was only produced in cultured spearmint shoots, and not in cultured spearmint roots or callus cultures. They also discovered that the growth and morphogenesis, as well as secondary metabolism, were significantly influenced by the number of media culture immersions with twelve immersions per day proving to be optimal for growth and morphogenesis.

\subsection*{Comparative Growth Studies}

\textcite{yaghobvand2022evaluation} tested 5 species of mints in an aeroponic system in greenhouse conditions. They found that \textit{M. spicata} had the biggest leaf-to-stem ratio. The majority of the yield-attributes, such as leaf area, leaf fresh and dry weight, root fresh weight, shoot fresh weight, and total plant dry weight and the other vegetative traits like relative water content and photosynthesis rate, were greater at the second harvest than at the first.

\subsection{\textit{Mentha crispata} (Curly Mint)}

Plants are hemisperious and feature 3-to 7-lobed leaves.

The \textcite{rhs2026crispa} stated that \textit{Mentha spicata} var. \textit{crispa} (curly spearmint) is an aromatic perennial which grows to 60 cm in height, spreading by creeping rhizomes. The leaves are bright green in colour, broad and crinkled. In summer tiny pale purple flowers are arranged in whorls at the ends of the spikes.

According to the \textcite{rhs2026crispa} there are multiple synonyms for this plant namely:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Mentha spicata} ’Crispa’
    \item Cristated Spearmint.
    \item \textit{Mentha crispa} L. (1753)
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Cultivation Requirements}

The \textcite{rhs2026crispa} advises that curly mint can be cultivated in any moist soil type such as chalk, clay, loam or sand. The soil should be moist, well drained but will tolerate poorly drained conditions. It is a plant that needs moist conditions but with well-drained soil and should not be allowed too much winter wet. It thrives in full sun to part shade and is best grown in west, south or east facing sites.

The \textcite{rhs2026crispa} states that in terms of growth management this plant may potentially become a nuisance. They advise limiting root run, using deep containers and placing in soil, and small contained beds. Dividing is beneficial for plants in containers every couple of years.

\section{Abiotic stress in mint cultivation with emphasis on salt stress}

According to \textcite{Hasanuzzaman2021}, salt stress is one of the most significant abiotic factors that limit the growth and productivity of plants, especially in controlled cultivation system where the salinity may accumulate in the root zone of the plants. As mentioned by \textcite{AcostaMotos2017}, salt stress negatively impacts the growth, plant photosynthetic pathway, and the production of secondary metabolites in medicinal and aromatic plants.

\section{The physiology and growth requirements of the mint species are discussed.}

Mint species need well-aerated substrates, sufficient water, and well-balanced nutrient content for optimal growth and biomass production \parencite{Zheljazkov2020}. \textcite{Singh2022} have reported that the physiological differences among the mint species determine their responses to environmental stresses.

\section{Nutrient solutions in soilless mint production.}

For mint production in soilless substrates, the substrates are mostly devoid of nutrients and the nutrient supply comes from the irrigation water, therefore defined nutrient solutions are applied, in which all essential plant nutrients are known to be present in a defined concentration. The most general form used is that of \textcite{Hoagland1950} which is based upon previous work by Hoagland and Snyder (1933) and Hoagland and Arnon (1938).

The solution serves as a source of macronutrients in the form of 4 salts: potassium nitrate, calcium nitrate, ammonium dihydrogen phosphate and magnesium sulfate, thus providing about 210 ppm nitrogen, 235 ppm potassium, 200 ppm calcium, 31 ppm phosphorus, 64 ppm sulfur and 49 ppm magnesium. Boric acid is added in case of micronutrients, along with manganese chloride, zinc sulfate, copper sulfate and sodium molybdate. Iron is supplied as ferric tartrate, but is now virtually always substituted by a chelated form like Fe-EDTA that is available to plants over a larger pH range.

As the original formulation was designed for sand and solution culture where the nutrient solution is continually changed, it is generally used at a diluted rate in container production using organic substrates where salts build up between irrigations. However, there are various modified versions of the solution, varying in their iron chelate, potassium:calcium ratio and the source of phosphate, and the term “modified Hoagland solution” is not a reference to one specific composition.

The Hoagland-type solutions are commonly used in salinity research in mint. For instance, \textcite{Morshedloo2025} provided 25\% Hoagland solution during the initial growth stage and then switched to full-strength Hoagland solution at the mature plant stage before applying salinity stress at 0, 50 and 100 mM NaCl.

\section{Effects of Salt stress on Medicinal and Spice plants}

In medicinal and spice crops, salt stress causes osmotic stress and ion toxicity and consequently, growth and yield reduction. The impact of these effects is sometimes more pronounced in container-grown crops because of the small root space \parencite{Hasanuzzaman2021}.

\section{Physiological responses of plants to salt stress}

\subsection{Chlorophyll content and photosynthesis}

The common effects of salinity stress are the decrease in chlorophyll content and photosynthetic efficiency due to the damage of chloroplasts and stomatal limitation \parencite{Kalaji2021}. These effects directly lead to a decrease of biomass under saline conditions \parencite{MunnsTester2008}.

\subsection{Water Relations and Osmotic Adjustments}

Salt stress affects the capacity to uptake water and reduces leaf water potential, inducing osmotic adjustment mechanisms including the increase of compatible solutes like \textcite{AcostaMotos2017}.

\section{Biochar effect on substrate properties and salinity stress}

Additionally, biochar application can enhance the water-holding capacity, cation exchange capacity, and nutrient retention in the growing media, thereby potentially mitigating salinity stress impacts on plants \parencite{Akhtar2021}. Furthermore, biochar can lower the availability of sodium ions to the root zone, alleviating ion toxicity in saline soil conditions \parencite{Joseph2021}.

\section{The identification of the gap in the study and the justification of the same.}

Based on the information provided in the previous sections it is clear that mint is an economically important crop, peat moss has important environmental limitations, biochar shows promise as a sustainable alternative, and salt stress is a real threat to greenhouse production. But there is one important knowledge gap: we don't know if biochar can make mint growers more salt tolerant. In particular, little research has been carried out on the effect of biochar amendment under the conditions of salt stress on various \textit{Mentha} species. There is a lack of systematic research comparing several different mint species to see if responses are species-dependent and/or if there are genotype differences in the benefits of biochar.

Evidence of these interactions is important to create grower recommendations for sustainable salt stress management in mint production. This gap gives rise to the need for the current study.

\section{Aim and objectives of the study}

The overall objective of this study is to see how biochar can be used as an alternative to peat and how it affects the salinity response of three mint species. Specific objectives were to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Evaluate growth response of various mint species to salt stress.
    \item Assess the effects of biochar incorporated growing media on salt stress tolerance.
    \item Compare physiological responses of different types of mints,
    \item Explain the difference in growth and physiological responses to salinity in unamended and biochar amended growing media.
\end{itemize}
