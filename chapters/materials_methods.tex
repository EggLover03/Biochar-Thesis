\chapter{Materials and Methods}

The experimental design and salt stress treatments were developed based on established methodologies used in 
previous biochar and salinity studies \textcite{Akhtar2021, Hasanuzzaman2021}.

\section{Experimental design}

\subsection{Growing media treatments}

Three growing media treatments were established for each mint species: an unamended peat control, and peat amended with either 15~g or 30~g of biochar per pot. Each treatment was replicated in three pots, giving nine pots per species and 27 pots in total across the three species (Table~\ref{tab:media}). The biochar was incorporated in a dry state, without prior soaking or pre-wetting.

All three species were potted on 15 September 2026.

\subsection{Salt stress treatments}

Two salinity levels were applied, 25~mM and 50~mM NaCl, alongside an unsalted treatment. Both solutions were prepared by dissolving sodium chloride in distilled water, in batches of 500~ml per concentration. The salinity levels fall within the range used in comparable work on mint: \textcite{Morshedloo2025} applied 0, 50 and 100~mM NaCl to \textit{Mentha} $\times$ \textit{gracilis}, while \textcite{Ounoki2021} used 0, 5, 25 and 50~mM over 14 days in spearmint.

Within each species the three pots prepared at a given biochar rate were each assigned a different salinity level, so that every biochar $\times$ salinity combination is represented by a single plant.

\section{Stress application procedure}

Salinity treatments were imposed on 29 September 2026, two weeks after potting. Application was deferred until the plants had established sufficient shoot growth, following the practice of \textcite{Morshedloo2025}, who withheld salinity until the six-leaf stage. Each pot received 100~ml of the appropriate NaCl solution; control pots received none.
