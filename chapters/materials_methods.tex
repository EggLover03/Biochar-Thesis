\chapter{Materials and Methods}

Experimental design and salt stress treatments were designed following the methodology already used in previous biochar and salinity studies by \textcite{Akhtar2021} and \textcite{Hasanuzzaman2021}.

\section{Experimental site and growth conditions}

The study was conducted in a growth chamber of a room in the faculty building.

The air humidity in the room was 60-70\% and the temperature was 26~$^{\circ}$C. The plants were cultivated under 12~h light and 12~h dark photoperiod, and under full spectrum light with 300~$\mu$mol~m$^{-2}$~s$^{-1}$ P.A.R.

\section{Plant material}

Three mint taxa were used. The species recognized are: \textit{Mentha piperita} L. (peppermint), \textit{Mentha crispata} (curly mint) and \textit{Mentha spicata} L. `Moroccan' (Moroccan mint).

Rooted plantlets of the plants were purchased from a commercial supplier and 3-4 plantlets were planted per pot. The supplier was the Budapest, Hungary company, Oázis Kertészet Kft. Plantlets were propagated by root shoots and the age of the shoots in months at planting was 2 - 3. The supplier has a plant certificate stating that the variety is a pure variety.

\section{Experimental design}

\subsection{Growing media treatments}

The three growing media treatments involved in the experiment were an unamended peat control, and peats amended with 15~g pot$^{-1}$ and 30~g pot$^{-1}$ of biochar respectively for each species of mint. The biochar produced is a commercial product (Carbozero ``Nature Carbon'', Pflanzenkohle) manufactured from wood biomass by multi-stage thermochemical conversion. It is produced by the company Energo Zlatá Olešnice s.r.o. (Zlatá Olešnice, Czech Republic) and sold by the company Dr.~Uhlí s.r.o. (Lučany nad Nisou, Czech Republic). Its specification from the manufacturer is as follows: At least 50\% carbon, at most 5\% moisture content, at most 15\% ash content, at most 10\% volatile matter content, at least pH 7, and at most molar H/C ratio 0.7 and O/C ratio 0.4. The declared upper limits for risk elements are 1~mg~kg$^{-1}$ of dry mass for cadmium, mercury and 10~mg~kg$^{-1}$ of dry mass for lead.

The concentration of arsenic is 20, of chromium 50 and of sum of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons 20. The biochar was added directly to the peat substrate without any soaking or pre-wetting, although the manufacturer recommends soaking the biochar in water for at least 48 hours prior to use and recommends mixing directly with substrate at a rate of 1 part biochar to 9 parts substrate by volume. One pot was used for each growing medium (unamended peat, 15~g and 30~g of biochar) with each of the three salinity levels (0, 25 and 50~mM NaCl) for each species of \textit{Mentha} (\textit{M. piperita}, \textit{M. crispata} and \textit{M. spicata} `Moroccan'). This gave a total of 9 pots per species and 27 pots in total (Table~\ref{tab:media}).

The amount of substrate in each pot was 400~g. For the biochar treatments, the peat was replaced by the same mass of biochar, thus 15~g of biochar was added to 385~g of peat (3.75\% biochar by mass) and 30~g of biochar to 370~g of peat (7.5\% biochar by mass). All three species were pot-ted on 15 September 2026.

The manufacturer does not specify the temperature used for pyrolysis of the material. However, the published specification is still indicative of a well done carbonisation process: a molar H/C ratio of at most 0.7 is a boundary value that is set by the European Biochar Certificate to distinguish between biochar and biomass that has not been well charred, and the low volatile matter content and the specific surface area of at least 150m$^2$~g$^{-1}$ all work in favour of a well done carbonisation process. The pyrolysis temperature is not given here as a value.

The declared limits for Cd, Hg, Pb, As, Cr and PAHs are the same as those used within the European Biochar Certificate, thus the biochar was produced in accordance to this standard.

\subsection{Salt stress treatments}

Three salinities were employed: 25~mM and 50~mM NaCl, and an unsalted control.

Both solutions prepared in distilled water containing sodium chloride at each concentration – 500 mls was used for each concentration. The salinity level was comparable to the other studies done with mint, where \textcite{Morshedloo2025} applied 0, 50 and 100~mM NaCl for 14 days to \textit{Mentha x gracilis} and \textcite{Ounoki2021} applied 0, 5, 25 and 50~mM NaCl to spearmint for 14 days.

For each species one pot was used for each growing medium x salinity combination, resulting in one pot per factorial combination.

\section{Cultivation practices}

\subsection{Nutrient solution}

The plants were grown in a modified Hoagland nutrient solution as per condition of \textcite{Hoagland1950}. Table~\ref{tab:hoagland} shows the composition of the full strength solution. The Fe-EDTA was substituted for the ferric tartrate in the original formulation and the pH of the solution was modified to approximately 6.5.

The nutrient solution was added twice a week (100~ml per pot) and distilled water was added twice a week (100~ml per pot). The electrical conductivity of the solution was 1.8 – 2~mS~cm$^{-1}$.

\subsection{Plant protection}

Aphid are pests that were observed during the growth stage, and were controlled by systemic neonicotinoid insecticide, Mospilan (acetamiprid) was applied. Therefore all plants in the experiment were sprayed on the foliage irrespective of growing medium or salinity treatment in order to ensure equal plant protection between treatments. Spraying was carried out on 29th September 2026, the day the salinity treatments were applied. The infestation was greater on \textit{M. spicata} `Moroccan' than on the other two.

Mospilan 20 SG was applied at 0,5 litres of water for 0,1~g. It was applied twice – the first in mid September, directly to the aphids, and the second a fortnight later at the end of September, to ward off re-infestation.

\section{Stress application procedure}

Salinity treatments were made 14 days after potting on 29th September, 2026. It was also used in the later stage after plants had started to show bigger shoot growth as it was also used by \textcite{Morshedloo2025} in salinity. The pots used per salinity treatment received 100~ml of the appropriate NaCl solution, while pots with the 0~mM salinity treatment (namely unamended peat) did not receive any NaCl solution. A salinity treatment was performed again 8 days later on 7 October 2026. The third spray of the NaCl solution was applied at the same rate on 13 October 2026. The electrical conductivity of the substrate and drainage water were not measured during the treatment period.

Therefore, the three applications of NaCl solution were given on 29 September, 7 October 2026 and 13 October 2026, with distances of 8 and 6 days respectively between applications, and plants were measured 4 weeks after the first application.

\section{Measured parameters}

\subsection{Growth parameters}

Four weeks after the first NaCl application, plant height and leaf length and width were measured using a digital caliper at the time of the measurement. The leaves sampled for measurements have been taken from the upper third of the plant as young leaves are the first to display stress symptoms. Each pot had three leaves measured and the three leaves were also measured for leaf length and width, Fv/Fm ratio and chlorophyll content.

\subsection{Physiological measurements}

Relative water content (RWC) was determined on one leaf of each pot using the fresh weight (FW), turgid weight (TW) and dry weight (DW) as:

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{RWC}\,(\%) = \frac{\mathrm{FW} - \mathrm{DW}}{\mathrm{TW} - \mathrm{DW}} \times 100
  \label{eq:rwc}
\end{equation}

The turgid weight was obtained by taking the leaf after they were soaked in distilled water for 24~h, and the dry weight was obtained after the leaf was dried at 60~$^{\circ}$C for 8~h. This was done for each pot, regardless of growing medium or salinity treatment.

FluorPen FP 100 was used to measure the Fv/Fm ratio following 30 minutes of dark adaptation. Chlorophyll content measured using Apogee MC-100 chlorophyll meter ($\mu$mol/m$^2$).

\subsection{Laboratory analyses}

All physiological measurements were taken using handheld instruments in the faculty's laboratory: Apogee chlorophyll meter MC-100, FluorPen FP 100. These were selected because they are easy to operate and provide good results when used on undamaged leaves, thus eliminating the need for extraction and spectrophotometric methods. The analysis of the content of essential oils was not carried out since such analysis is unavailable at the faculty.

\section{Statistical analysis}

Three leaf readings were made on each of three leaves sampled from each pot using the handheld instruments. Each leaf was read three times – analytical replicates – to improve the accuracy of the characterisation of that leaf – and the three readings were averaged to arrive at one reading per leaf for each measured parameter. The leaves from one pot are not independent replicates of the pot, but are subsamples of the pot because they have the same substrate and the same treatment. One pot per growing medium-salinity combination was used.

With the three leaf values averaged in turn, the pot was used as the unit of analysis, with one pot per treatment combination. Two-way analysis of variance with the given interaction between biochar and salinity as error term (4 d.f.) and Tukey's test for pairwise comparisons were used to test the effects of the biochar rate (0, 15 and 30~g) and salinity level (0, 25 and 50~mM NaCl), respectively, for each species. The significance level used was 0.05. The calculations were done in Python (SciPy). One leaf from each pot was used for fresh and turgid and dry weights but these were not analysed statistically. Since there is no replication, the tests are exploratory.

Due to limited duplication between pots the differences between treatments should be used with caution.
