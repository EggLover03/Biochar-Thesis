\chapter{Materials and Methods}

The experimental design and salt stress treatments were developed based on established methodologies used in 
previous biochar and salinity studies \textcite{Akhtar2021, Hasanuzzaman2021}.

\section{Plant material}

\section{Experimental design}

\subsection{Growing media treatments}

Three growing media treatments were established for each mint species: an unamended peat control, and peat amended with either 15~g or 30~g of biochar per pot. The biochar was incorporated in a dry state, without prior soaking or pre-wetting. Each growing medium was combined with each of three salinity levels, giving nine pots per species and 27 pots in total across the three species (Table~\ref{tab:media}).

All three species were potted on 15 September 2026.

\subsection{Salt stress treatments}

Two salinity levels were applied, 25~mM and 50~mM NaCl, alongside an unsalted treatment. Both solutions were prepared by dissolving sodium chloride in distilled water, in batches of 500~ml per concentration. The salinity levels fall within the range used in comparable work on mint: \textcite{Morshedloo2025} applied 0, 50 and 100~mM NaCl to \textit{Mentha} $\times$ \textit{gracilis}, while \textcite{Ounoki2021} used 0, 5, 25 and 50~mM over 14 days in spearmint.

Each of the three growing media was combined with each of the three salinity levels, so that every growing medium $\times$ salinity combination is represented by a single plant in each species.

\subsection{Plant protection}

An aphid infestation occurred during the growing period and was treated with Mospilan, a systemic neonicotinoid insecticide with acetamiprid as its active ingredient. The product was applied as a foliar spray to every plant in the experiment, irrespective of growing medium or salinity treatment, so that plant protection was uniform across all treatments.

\section{Stress application procedure}

Salinity treatments were imposed on 29 September 2026, two weeks after potting. Application was deferred until the plants had established sufficient shoot growth, following the practice of \textcite{Morshedloo2025}, who withheld salinity until the six-leaf stage. Each pot assigned to a salinity treatment received 100~ml of the appropriate NaCl solution, including the pots in unamended peat; pots at the 0~mM level received none.

\section{Statistical analysis}

Three leaves were measured on each plant. These constitute analytical replicates: they improve the precision with which each plant is characterised by averaging out variation between individual leaves, and were therefore averaged to give a single value per plant for each measured parameter.

Because every combination of growing medium and salinity was represented by one plant within each species, the design contains no biological replication. Variation between individual plants of the same treatment cannot be estimated, and inferential statistics such as analysis of variance were therefore not applied. Results are reported as descriptive statistics, and differences between treatments are described as observed trends rather than tested for statistical significance.
