\chapter{Materials and Methods}

The experimental design and salt stress treatments were developed based on established methodologies used in 
previous biochar and salinity studies \textcite{Akhtar2021, Hasanuzzaman2021}.

\section{Experimental site and growth conditions}

\section{Plant material}

Three mint taxa were used: \textit{Mentha piperita} L. (peppermint), \textit{Mentha crispata} (curly mint) and \textit{Mentha spicata} L. `Moroccan' (Moroccan mint).

The plants were purchased from a commercial supplier as rooted plantlets, and three to four plantlets were planted in each pot.

\section{Experimental design}

\subsection{Growing media treatments}

Three growing media treatments were established for each mint species: an unamended peat control, and peat amended with either 15~g or 30~g of biochar per pot. The biochar was a commercial wood-derived product (Carbozero ``Nature Carbon'', produced by Energo Zlat\'a Ole\v{s}nice s.r.o., Czech Republic), made from wood biomass by a multi-stage thermochemical conversion process. According to the manufacturer's specification it has a carbon content of at least 50\%, a moisture content of at most 5\%, an ash content of at most 15\%, a volatile matter content of at most 10\%, a pH of at least 7, a specific surface area of at least \SI{150}{\metre\squared\per\gram}, and molar H/C and O/C ratios of at most 0.7 and 0.4, respectively. The biochar was mixed through the peat and incorporated in a dry state, without prior soaking or pre-wetting, although the manufacturer recommends soaking it in water for at least 48 hours before application. For \textit{M. piperita} and \textit{M. crispata}, each biochar rate (15~g and 30~g) was combined with each of three salinity levels (0, 25 and 50~mM NaCl), giving three pots per rate, and one further pot was grown in unamended peat without salt (0~mM). For \textit{M. spicata} `Moroccan', each biochar rate was combined with the 25 and 50~mM salinity levels only, and one pot was grown in unamended peat without salt (0~mM). This gave seven pots for each of \textit{M. piperita} and \textit{M. crispata}, five pots for \textit{M. spicata} `Moroccan', and 19 pots in total (Table~\ref{tab:media}).

All three species were potted on 15 September 2026.

\subsection{Salt stress treatments}

Two salinity levels were applied, 25~mM and 50~mM NaCl, alongside an unsalted treatment. Both solutions were prepared by dissolving sodium chloride in distilled water, in batches of 500~ml per concentration. The salinity levels fall within the range used in comparable work on mint: \textcite{Morshedloo2025} applied 0, 50 and 100~mM NaCl to \textit{Mentha} $\times$ \textit{gracilis}, while \textcite{Ounoki2021} used 0, 5, 25 and 50~mM over 14 days in spearmint.

Each growing medium $\times$ salinity combination that was established is represented by a single pot within each species. The design is not a complete factorial: \textit{M. spicata} `Moroccan' has no biochar pots at 0~mM, and each species has only one unamended-peat pot.

\subsection{Plant protection}

An aphid infestation occurred during the growing period and was treated with Mospilan, a systemic neonicotinoid insecticide with acetamiprid as its active ingredient. The product was applied as a foliar spray to every plant in the experiment, irrespective of growing medium or salinity treatment, so that plant protection was uniform across all treatments. The spray was applied on 29 September 2026, the same day on which the salinity treatments were imposed. The infestation was more severe on \textit{M. spicata} `Moroccan' than on the other two species.

\section{Stress application procedure}

Salinity treatments were imposed on 29 September 2026, two weeks after potting. Application was deferred until the plants had established sufficient shoot growth, following the practice of \textcite{Morshedloo2025}, who withheld salinity until the six-leaf stage. Each pot assigned to a salinity treatment received 100~ml of the appropriate NaCl solution, whereas pots at the 0~mM level, including those in unamended peat, received none. The salinity treatment was repeated once, on 7 October 2026, eight days after the first application. The electrical conductivity of the substrate and of the drainage water was not monitored during the treatment period.

\section{Statistical analysis}

Three leaves were measured on each plant. These constitute analytical replicates: they improve the precision with which each plant is characterised by averaging out variation between individual leaves, and were therefore averaged to give a single value per plant for each measured parameter.

Because each combination of growing medium and salinity was represented by one pot within each species, the design contains no replication at the level of the experimental unit. The three to four plants within a pot share the same substrate, treatment and growing conditions, so they are subsamples rather than independent replicates, and variation between pots of the same treatment cannot be estimated, and inferential statistics such as analysis of variance were therefore not applied. Results are reported as descriptive statistics, and differences between treatments are described as observed trends rather than tested for statistical significance.
