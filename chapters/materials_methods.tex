\chapter{Materials and Methods}

The experimental design and salt stress treatments were developed based on established methodologies used in previous biochar and salinity studies \textcite{Akhtar2021, Hasanuzzaman2021}.

\section{Experimental site and growth conditions}

\section{Plant material}

Three mint taxa were used: \textit{Mentha piperita} L. (peppermint), \textit{Mentha crispata} (curly mint) and \textit{Mentha spicata} L. `Moroccan' (Moroccan mint).

The plants were bought from a commercial supplier as rooted plantlets, with 3 – 4 plantlets planted in each pot.

\section{Experimental design}

\subsection{Growing media treatments}

Each species of mint was grown under three growing media treatments: an unamended peat control and peat amended with 15 or 30 g pot$^{-1}$ of biochar. The biochar used is a commercial product (Carbozero “Nature Carbon”) produced from wood biomass using a multi-stage thermochemical conversion. The manufacturer's specification is: its carbon content is at least 50\%, moisture content is at most 5\%, ash content is at most 15\%, volatile matter content is at most 10\%, the pH value is at least 7, the specific surface area at least 150 m$^2$ g$^{-1}$, and the molar H/C and O/C ratios are at most 0.7 and 0.4, respectively. The biochar was incorporated in the peat in dry form, without soaking or pre-wetting although the manufacturer suggests soaking for at least 48 hours in water prior to use. Each growing medium (unamended peat, 15 g and 30 g of biochar) was coupled with each of the three salinity levels (0, 25 and 50 mM NaCl) for \textit{M. piperita}, \textit{M. crispata} and \textit{M. spicata} ‘Moroccan’, resulting in one pot for each growing medium and salinity level. This resulted in nine pots for each species and 27 pots overall (Table~\ref{tab:media}).

On 15 September 2026 all three species were potted.

\subsection{Salt stress treatments}

Three levels of salinity were used, 25 mM and 50 mM NaCl and an unsalted control. Both solutions were made by dissolving sodium chloride in distilled water, with the concentrations made in 500 mls at each concentration. Salinity concentrations were similar to those applied in other studies involving mint: \textcite{Morshedloo2025} applied 0, 50 and 100 mM NaCl in \textit{Mentha} × \textit{gracilis} and \textcite{Ounoki2021} applied 0, 5, 25 and 50 mM NaCl to spearmint for a duration of 14 days.

For each species, one pot of each growing medium × salinity combination was set up, giving a complete factorial design with one pot per combination.

\subsection{Plant protection}

Aphid infestation was recorded during the growth period and the systemic neonicotinoid insecticide, Mospilan containing acetamiprid as active ingredients, was used for control. Thus, foliar spray of this product was applied to all plants in the experiment regardless of growing medium or salinity treatment to provide an equal level of plant protection across treatments. The spray was applied on 29 September 2026, the same day the salinity treatments were applied. Infestation was more severe on \textit{M. spicata} ‘Moroccan’ than on the other two.

\section{Stress application procedure}

Salinity treatments were applied on 29th September 2026, 14 days after potting. It was also applied at the later stages when the plants had developed larger shoot growth as done by \textcite{Morshedloo2025} on salinity. The pots for each salinity treatment were filled with 100 ml of the correct NaCl solution; pots in the 0 mM treatment (including unamended peat) were not filled with NaCl solution. This salinity treatment was replicated 8 days later on 7 October 2026. One more application of the NaCl solution was made on 13 October 2026. During the treatment period, the electrical conductivity of the substrate and of the drainage water was not measured.

\section{Statistical analysis}

Triplicate leaves were sampled from each pot and three readings were taken on each leaf in the spectrophotometer. The three readings for a leaf were analytical replicates, used to increase the accuracy of the characterisation of that leaf; they were averaged together to provide one reading per leaf for each measured parameter. The leaves collected from a single pot are subsampled from that pot as the treatment is the same for all of the leaves and the substrate.

Each growing medium and salinity combination had only one pot and the leaves sampled from a pot were subsamples of the pot. Description and comparisons of treatments within species were based on leaf values. Due to limited replication, results differences between treatments should be interpreted with caution as the replication was limited to the leaves in each pot.
