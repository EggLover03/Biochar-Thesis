\chapter{Introduction}

The medicinal and aromatic plants (MAPs) are an important group of crops which are traditionally used in the field of medicine and food production, cosmetics and pharmaceutical industries due to the high amount of bioactive compounds \parencite{Ahmad2020}. The essential oils, the phenolic compounds, and the terpenoids of these plants are among the secondary metabolites that have antimicrobial, antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties \parencite{Ahmad2020}. The cultivation of medicinal and aromatic plants has also greatly increased over the last few decades, especially in view of the growing demand for natural products and medicinal herbs from various plants around the world \parencite{Ahmad2020}.

\textit{Mentha spp.} (mints) are medicinal and aromatic crops widely grown and economically important herbs from Lamiaceae family \parencite{Kalemba2019}. Mint species are important raw materials of the food, cosmetic and pharmaceutical industry due to their high biomass production and also due to the presence of essential oils \parencite{Zheljazkov2010}. Some of the biologically active compounds of the essential oils extracted from mint plants are menthol, menthone and carvone \textcite{Ahmad2020} which gives the characteristic aroma, flavor and therapeutic properties. These compounds have been extensively employed in flavoring beverages, confectionery product and oral hygiene product, as well as medicinal preparations \textcite{Ahmad2020}. In the genus \textit{Mentha piperita} (peppermint), there are many species of which \textit{Mentha spicata} (spearmint) and \textit{Mentha crispata} are are thought to be the most important due to their high essential oil yields and the extensive commercial cultivation \parencite{Kalemba2019}. Peppermint is a hybrid variety of \textit{Mentha aquatica} and \textit{Mentha spicata} and is grown for oil rich in menthol and menthone conferring cooling effect and medicinal properties to peppermint \textcite{Ahmad2020}. Spearmint is rich in the essential oil component carvone and is commonly used in food preparations and as a component of herbal preparations \parencite{Zheljazkov2010}. Because of their chemical composition and biological activities, these mint species are important for agriculture production and on the international trade market of herbs \textcite{Kalemba2019}.

Currently, medicinal and aromatic plants are often cultivated under glasshouse and/or protected environment to ensure the yield and quality of the crops produced \parencite{Zheljazkov2020}. In such systems, the media in which plants are cultivated is a very important factor in the plant's growth and development. Peat moss has long been among the most popular ingredients of substrates because of its desirable properties, including a high water holding capacity, low bulk density and good aeration \parencite{Dumroese2018}. However, peat harvesting has caused major environmental concerns; peatlands are large reservoirs of carbon and degradation can cause the release of stored carbon into the atmosphere \parencite{Cleary2021}. Therefore, it has been attempted to identify other materials which could be used on a sustainable basis as growing media in addition to peat. Biochar has emerged as an interesting alternative to the components of substrates applied in sustainable agriculture and horticulture in recent years \parencite{Dumroese2018}.

\textcite{Joseph2021} proposed the production of biochar, which is a carbon rich material with limited oxygen pyrolysed organic biomass. The porous structure, large surface area, and high CEC of biochar can also be utilized to enhance the physical and chemical properties of growing media including water holding capacity, nutrient availability and microbial activity, according to \textcite{Joseph2021}. In addition to improving the soil properties, biochar has also been reported to improve the environmental stress in plants like salinity stress \textcite{Farhangi2017}.

Salinity stress is one of the most critical abiotic stress which affects plant growth and agriculture production in the world according to \textcite{Volkov2014}. High levels of soluble salts in the root zone will reduce water uptake by plants, result in ionic toxicity and macro and microelement imbalance \textcite{Volkov2014}. The accumulation of sodium ions in the tissues of plants may lead to a violation of metabolic processes, enzyme activity and photosynthesis, which will cause inhibition of plant growth and biomass \parencite{Volkov2014}. Growth of medicinal and aromatic plants (MAPs), which are sensitive to environmental stresses, is affected by salinity which can result in reduced growth and synthesis of secondary metabolites responsible for the plant medicinal value \textcite{Kalemba2019}.

Recently, it has been suggested that biochar application can mitigate the negative effects of salinity both by improving soil properties and reducing the amount of sodium available in the root zone \textcite{Farhangi2017}. \textcite{Akhtar2021} suggests that biochar amendments may alleviate salt stress by enhancing soil's water holding capacity, availability of nutrients and favorable microbial activity. \textcite{Joseph2021} reported that biochar amendments' effects on plants vary depending on the plant species, biochar properties, and environmental context. Despite the potential of biochar to affect growth and essential oil yield in mint \parencite{Zheljazkov2019}, there is limited knowledge about the effect of biochar on the salinity stress response of different mint species.

Therefore it is important to understand the interactive influence of growing media and salinity stress on plants for more sustainable cultivation of medicinal and aromatic plants. Thus, in this context, it is meaningful to compare the response of the salt stress of three mint species \textit{Mentha piperita}, \textit{Mentha spicata} and \textit{Mentha crispata} to biochar use as an alternative to peat-based growing media. To assess how saline conditions impact on plant growth, plant physiology and whether biochar can enhance plant salt stress tolerance and serve as a safe and environmentally friendly plant substrate.
