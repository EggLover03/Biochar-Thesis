\chapter{Introduction}

The medicinal and aromatic plants (MAPs) are an important group of crops which are traditionally used in the field of medicine and food production, as well as cosmetics and pharmaceutical industries because of the high content of bioactive compounds \parencite{Ahmad2020}. Such secondary metabolites include the essential oils, the phenolic compounds, and the terpenoids of these plants, which possess antimicrobial, antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties \parencite{Ahmad2020}. Considering the rising demand for natural products and medicinal herbs derived from plants worldwide in recent decades, there has been a significant rise in the growing of medicinal and aromatic plants \parencite{Ahmad2020}.

Plants of the \textit{Mentha} genus (mints) are medicinal and aromatic crops that are widely grown and economically important herbs in the family Lamiaceae \parencite{Kalemba2019}. Mint species are important raw materials in the food, cosmetic and pharmaceutical industries because they are high biomass producers and contain essential oils \parencite{Zheljazkov2010}. Some of the biologically active compounds of the essential oils extracted from mint plants are menthol, menthone and carvone \parencite{Ahmad2020} that give their characteristic aroma, flavor and therapeutic properties. These compounds are used broadly as flavouring agents in beverages, confectionery products, oral hygiene products and medicinal preparations \parencite{Ahmad2020}. Of the many species in the genus \textit{Mentha piperita} (peppermint), \textit{Mentha spicata} (spearmint), and \textit{Mentha crispata} are believed to be the most significant because of their high essential oil yields and wide commercial cultivation \parencite{Kalemba2019}. Peppermint is a hybrid species of \textit{Mentha aquatica} and \textit{Mentha spicata} and is cultivated for its oil which is high in menthol and menthone that give the cooling effect and medicinal properties to peppermint \parencite{Ahmad2020}. Spearmint can be characterized by the presence of the essential oil component, carvone, which is present in high amounts, and it is widely used in culinary products and in herbal formulations \parencite{Zheljazkov2010}. These mint species are important for agricultural production and on the international trade market of herbs due to their chemical composition and biological activities \parencite{Kalemba2019}.

Today, medicinal and aromatic plants are frequently grown under glasshouse and/or protected environment to guarantee the yield and quality of produced crops \parencite{Zheljazkov2020}. In these systems, the choice of growing media is critical for the growth and development of plants. Due to its desirable characteristics, such as high water holding capacity, low bulk density and good aeration, peat moss has long been one of the most widely used substrate ingredients \parencite{Dumroese2018}. Peat extraction, however, has led to significant environmental worries as peatlands are significant carbon stores and degradation can result in release of stored carbon into the atmosphere \parencite{Cleary2021}. As a result, efforts have been made to find alternative materials to peat that can be used sustainably as growing media.

In recent years, biochar has become an attractive alternative to the components of substrates, which are traditionally used in sustainable agriculture and horticulture \parencite{Dumroese2018}. The production of biochar, a carbon rich material that is created by pyrolysing organic biomass under limited oxygen, was suggested by \textcite{Joseph2021}. The porous structure, large surface area, and high CEC of biochar can also be used to improve the physical and chemical properties of growing media, such as water holding capacity, nutrient availability and microbial activity \parencite{Joseph2021}. Apart from enhancing the soil properties, biochar has also been reported to relieve environmental stress in plants such as salinity stress \parencite{Farhangi2017}. Salinity stress is known as one of the leading abiotic stresses that restrict plant growth and agricultural productivity at global level \parencite{Volkov2014}.

Excess soluble salts in the root zone will decrease water absorption by plants, lead to ionic toxicity and imbalance of macro and micro elements \parencite{Volkov2014}. Sodium ions accumulating in plant tissues could disrupt metabolic processes, enzyme activity and photosynthesis, resulting in plant growth and biomass reduction \parencite{Volkov2014}. Salinity is one of the major factors affecting the growth of medicinal and aromatic plants, which are sensitive to environmental stress, which can lead to reduced growth and synthesis of secondary metabolites that are responsible for plant medicinal value \parencite{Kalemba2019}.

It has been recently proposed that biochar application may alleviate the adverse impacts of salinity through enhancement of soil properties and decrease the availability of sodium in the root zone \parencite{Farhangi2017}. Biochar amendments can help mitigate salt stress by improving water retention, increasing nutrient availability, and promoting beneficial microbial activity in the soil \parencite{Akhtar2021}. The plant species, biochar properties, and environmental context all affect the response of plants to biochar amendments \parencite{Joseph2021}. While biochar has demonstrated ability to influence growth and essential oil yield in mint \parencite{Zheljazkov2019}, little information is available on the effects of biochar on the response of different mint species to salinity stress.

Hence, it is significant to gain insight into the combined effect of growing media composition and salinity stress to develop sustainable cultivation methods for medicinal and aromatic plants. Therefore, in this context, the present study compares the responses to salt stress of three mint species, \textit{Mentha piperita}, \textit{Mentha spicata} and \textit{Mentha crispata}, grown in peat amended with 15~g or 30~g of biochar per pot as an alternative to peat-based growing media. The aim of the study is to determine the effect of saline conditions (0, 25 and 50~mM NaCl) on plant growth and physiological responses, and to determine whether biochar can increase plant tolerance to salt stress and be used as a safe and eco-friendly plant substrate.
