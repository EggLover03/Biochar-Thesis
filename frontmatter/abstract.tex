\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Peat is the main constituent of horticultural growing media, but its extraction releases stored carbon and degrades peatland habitats, and biochar has been proposed as a partial replacement. Salinity is a further constraint on protected mint production. This study examined the effect of biochar as a peat alternative on the salt stress responses of three mint taxa, \textit{Mentha piperita}, \textit{Mentha crispata} and \textit{Mentha spicata} `Moroccan'.

The plants were grown in a growth chamber in 27 pots, one for each combination of taxon, growing medium and salinity level, each pot holding 400~g of substrate and three to four plants. Each taxon was grown in unamended peat and in peat in which 15 or 30~g of the peat had been replaced by biochar, and received 0, 25 or 50~mM NaCl once a week for three weeks. Four weeks after the first application, plant height, leaf length and width, the Fv/Fm ratio, the chlorophyll content and the relative water content were measured on leaves taken from the upper third of the plants. The results were examined within each taxon by two-way analysis of variance on the pot means.

Salinity reduced plant height, leaf size and chlorophyll content in all three taxa. In unamended peat, plant height at 50~mM was 31\% lower than at 0~mM in \textit{M. piperita}, 44\% lower in \textit{M. crispata} and 51\% lower in \textit{M. spicata} `Moroccan', which was the most sensitive taxon. The Fv/Fm ratio decreased only slightly. Biochar increased plant height and leaf size at every salinity level and reduced the loss of growth and chlorophyll caused by salt, the effect being greater with 30~g than with 15~g and in the more sensitive taxa. The relative water content could not be interpreted and is excluded from the conclusions.

Biochar can therefore partly replace peat in the growing media of mint and can reduce the effects of salt stress. Because only one pot was available for each combination, the findings are preliminary and should be confirmed in replicated experiments.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} biochar, peat alternative, salinity, \textit{Mentha}, growing media, salt stress
