\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

The biochar has been suggested as an alternative for peat in horticultural growing media, although the extraction of peat releases stored carbon and harms peatland habitats. Another limitation to protected mint production is salinity. The aim of this study was to investigate effect of biochar as peat alternative on salt stress responses of three mint taxa \textit{Mentha piperita}, \textit{Mentha crispata} and \textit{Mentha spicata} `Moroccan'.

The experiment was conducted in a growth chamber with the plants in 27 pots (400~g of substrate and 3-4 plants per pot) representing each taxon, growing medium and salinity level. Each taxon was cultured in unamended peat and peat containing 15 or 30~g of biochar per 400~g of substrate and received 0, 25 or 50~mM of NaCl on three occasions over a fortnight. Leaf relative water content, chlorophyll content, the Fv/Fm ratio, plant height and leaf length and width were measured 4 weeks after the first application, using leaves from the upper third of the plants. Two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) on pot means was used to evaluate the results at each taxon level.

Plant height, leaf size and chlorophyll content were all decreased with salinity for each of the three taxa. The effects of unamended peat on height were measured at 50~mM and were 31\% lower compared to 0~mM in \textit{M. piperita}, 44\% lower in \textit{M. crispata} and 51\% lower in \textit{M. spicata} `Moroccan' (most sensitive taxon). There was a slight decrease in the Fv/Fm ratio. Biochar boosted plant height and leaf size at each salinity level and minimized salinity-induced growth and chlorophyll loss, with higher effect of 30~g compared to 15~g and in more salt sensitive taxa. The relative water content could not be interpreted and therefore it is not included in the conclusions.

Biochar is thus able to partially substitute the peat from the growing media for mint cultivation and mitigate the impacts of salt stress. Only one pot per combination was available and findings are preliminary and will need to be confirmed in repeat trials. \textbf{Key words:} biochar, peat alternative, salinity, \textit{Mentha}, growing media, salt stress.
